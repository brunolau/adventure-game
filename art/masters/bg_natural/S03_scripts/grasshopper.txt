Add one small wooden playground figure to the log playground at the far left of the picture: a cartoon grasshopper ("lucny konik", the meadow grasshopper the playground is named after), built by a village carpenter from plain squared timber - simple blocky carpentry, not a sculpture.

Where: in the narrow gap between the small log play tower with the shingle roof and the shelter's front-left log post, i.e. between about 9 % and 13 % from the left edge, standing on the meadow right behind the green slide. Seen exactly side-on, facing left towards the play tower. Its feet stand on the grass behind the slide at about 54 % from the top, so the green slide stays in front of it and hides its feet and lower legs. Its body is at about 45 % from the top, the knees of its hind legs and its head at about 40 %, the tips of its antennae at about 35 % from the top. It is about as tall as the play tower's posts and fits completely inside the gap: nothing of it overlaps the play tower, the slide, the standing stone or the shelter post.

What it looks like: a long body made of one thick squared beam, slightly tilted up towards the head; a box-shaped head with two big round eyes (white discs with black pupils); two thin curved antennae of bent steel rod; two long hind legs made of planks folded in a sharp zig-zag with the knees standing up above the body (the typical grasshopper silhouette); short front legs; a few round wooden climbing pegs on its side. Natural honey-coloured timber like the play tower, the head and the hind-leg thighs painted grass green, visible bolts at the joints. Its silhouette stands clearly against the sunny fields behind it.

The play tower, the green slide, the standing stone, the balance beam, the shelter post, the roof and the trees stay exactly as they are. No other figures, no signs, no text.
